# Bakersfield people (person): research notes

Phase P2, writer session 2026-10-04. **The first use of `"level": "person"`.** 6 cards written,
one per person, before the §9 check.

One notes file covers all six rather than six near-identical files, because the useful part of the
record is the shared judgement call: who cleared the §3a bar, who did not, and why.

## The bar as applied
§3a asks two questions: would a listener recognise the name, or is the reason interesting on its
own; and is the tie to this place real — born here, raised here, or did the work here. The
candidate list came from Wikipedia's *List of people from Bakersfield, California*, which runs to
several hundred names, and then each candidate's own article was read for the tie.

## In (6 cards)
| Person | Tie | Why a listener cares |
|---|---|---|
| Buck Owens | Did the work here; settled 1951 | 21 number-one country hits; *Hee Haw* |
| Merle Haggard | Born Oildale 1937 | 38 number-one country hits; born in a boxcar |
| Earl Warren | Raised here from 1896 | Chief Justice of the United States; *Brown v. Board* |
| Rick Mears | Raised here | Four Indianapolis 500 wins, record six poles |
| Marc Davis | Born here 1913 | Drew Tinker Bell, Cruella de Vil, Maleficent |

All six are anchored on Bakersfield, 35.373333, -119.018889, radius 15,000 m, with
`anchor` `{"type": "place", "name": "Bakersfield"}`, matching the town deck.

## Out, and why
- **Kevin McCarthy** (former Speaker of the House) and **Vince Fong** (congressman). Both living,
  both currently political. §7's `voting` tag exists for election results, not for a career
  summary, and §3a says nothing a person would resent hearing said about them to strangers in a car.
  A card about a sitting or recent politician cannot be written without it reading as a position.
  Left out on the "would need a lawyer's eye" test.
- **George H. W. Bush and George W. Bush.** The list has them at a Bakersfield house in 1949.
  "Lived here briefly" is exactly the tie §3a rules out.
- **Lawrence Tibbett**, opera singer, born in Bakersfield 1896. Genuine star in his day; almost
  nobody in a car in 2026 knows the name, and the reason — he sang opera — is not interesting on
  its own. Out on the recognition bar. Reconsider only with a hook (he was a film star too, and
  the first American-trained singer to headline at the Met, if that can be sourced).
- **Frank Gifford.** Pro Football Hall of Fame and a famous broadcaster, so he clears recognition
  easily, but he was born in Santa Monica. The Bakersfield list includes him and he went to
  Bakersfield High School, but no fetched source plainly says he was raised here, and §3a says if
  the article does not plainly say they are from this place there is no card. **Worth one fetch
  next time**; if the tie holds he belongs in the deck.
- **David Carr and Derek Carr**, NFL quarterbacks, and **Jordan Love**, and **Kevin Harvick**
  (NASCAR champion), and **Casey Mears**. Strong candidates on recognition. Left out this session
  to avoid five sports cards in one small city, and because Rick Mears already carries the motor
  racing slot and is the best of them for a driving audience. Harvick and Derek Carr are the two to
  add first if the density in the drive feels thin.
- **Carver Mead**, pioneer of very-large-scale integrated circuit design. This is the "person who
  invented the thing in your glovebox" case §3a is written for and he may well be the best card
  left. Not written because his tie to Bakersfield was not confirmed from his own article. **Top of
  the list for next session.**
- **Hans Einstein**, the leading authority on valley fever. Same category; the tie needs checking.
- **Gregory Porter**, jazz singer, Grammy winner, raised in Bakersfield. Plausible; not fetched.
- **Tyrus Wong**, the artist behind the look of *Bambi*. Extraordinary story, but the list says he
  married in Bakersfield, which is not a tie.
- **Poy Gum Lee**, architect: died in Bakersfield. Not a tie.
- **Vincent Brothers, Rodolfo Cadena, Steven David Catlin.** The list's crime section. Rejected
  outright: three murderers is not what a town deck should sound like, and §3a's purpose is to tell
  a passenger whose home town they are driving through, not to recite its killers. If any crime card
  is ever wanted for Bakersfield it belongs in a town card with the `crime` tag, not a biography.
- **Justin Berry.** On the list with a description no road narration should repeat. Out.
- **Bill Woods, Wynn Stewart, Red Simpson, Bonnie Owens, Fuzzy Owen.** The long tail of the
  Bakersfield Sound. All real, all below the recognition bar, and Bill Woods's claim to have
  organised the scene is interesting mostly to people who already know the story. §3a's warning
  about "dead air with a name in it" is aimed squarely at this group. Out.
- **Adema, Burning Image, Brandon Cruz, Ric Drasin, Marc Davis's co-animators.** Out.

- **Earl Warren** (rev 1378499611) — born Los Angeles 19 March 1891; "In 1896, the family resettled

  graduated from Kern County High School in 1908"; "He was the first Governor of California elected
  to three consecutive terms"; Chief Justice 1953 to 1969; "helped arrange a unanimous decision in

- **Rick Mears** (rev 1372423395) — "Mears was born in Wichita, Kansas and raised in Bakersfield,

  record six poles; three Indycar titles, 1979, 1981, 1982; began in off-road racing.
- **Marc Davis** (rev 1374059112) — "Marc Fraser Davis (March 30, 1913 - January 12, 2000) was an
  American artist and animator who was one of Disney's Nine Old Men"; "Davis was born in
  Bakersfield, California, on March 30, 1913"; Cruella de Vil, Tinker Bell, Maleficent, Aurora;
  Pirates of the Caribbean (1967) and The Haunted Mansion (1969); Walt Disney's "He's my
  Renaissance man".
- **Jonathan Davis** (rev 1377474946) — "Jonathan Howsmon Davis was born in Bakersfield,
  California, on January 18, 1971"; raised in Bakersfield by his father; "At the age of 16, Davis
  found employment as a coroner's assistant"; an apprenticeship at the Kern County Coroner's
  Department; "Davis co-founded Korn in Bakersfield in 1993"; the first Korn song to feature a
  Highland bagpipe.

## Living-people restraint, applied
- **Jonathan Davis** is alive. The card gives what he is known for, where he is from, the coroner's
  job and the bagpipes. Left out deliberately: the harassment he describes at school, his mother and
  stepmother, and everything about his health. All of it is in the article; none of it belongs in a
  car.
- **Rick Mears** is alive. The card is entirely race record and birthplace.
- **Merle Haggard's San Quentin sentence and Reagan pardon are in the card** and it carries the
  `crime` tag. He died in 2016, it is the best known fact about him after the music, he sang about
  it himself, and the pardon is the point of the sentence. If the owner wants it out, the card still
  stands on the boxcar and the 38 hits.
- **Earl Warren's card** names *Brown v. Board of Education* and the Kennedy commission. Both are
  stated as what he did, with no comment. No `sensitive` tag was applied; the tags in §7 cover
  election results and party politics, not a court's decisions. Flagging for the checker in case the
  owner would rather have `tragedy` on the Kennedy clause.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Earl Warren: "first" or "only"?** One fetch of the article returned "the only governor of
  California to be elected for three consecutive terms"; the verbatim-focused fetch returned "He was
  the first Governor of California elected to three consecutive terms." The card uses "first", which
  is the quote actually in hand and the weaker claim.
- **Haggard's birthplace is Oildale, not Bakersfield.** The card is anchored on Bakersfield with a
  15,000 m radius and says "in Oildale, just outside Bakersfield", following the precedent set in
  the Keene notes for a place with no citable coordinates. No fetched page gives Oildale's
  coordinates, so it could not be an `elsewhere`. The Kern County Museum is the `elsewhere` instead,
  for the boxcar's present home, which is the part of the card that is genuinely away from the road.
- **Checotah, Oklahoma was cut** from the Haggard card and replaced with "Oklahoma". The town name
  carries no part of the story and it hands the voice a word it will mangle. §4's "leave the old
  spelling alone" test applied to a place name.
- **Buck Owens was born in Texas, not here.** The card says so in its second sentence, and leads on
  the 21 number ones "out of Bakersfield", which is the real tie — he did the work here.
- Every card rests on one Wikipedia article, so confidence is 4 at best. For biography that is
  normal and §5 allows it; nothing in any of the six cards is a surprising claim needing two sources,
  with the possible exception of Mears's pole-position record, which the article itself states as a
  current record with all six years listed.
