# Bena (town): research notes

Phase P2, writer session 2026-10-04. 2 cards written, before the §9 check.

## Is there real material here?
Barely. Bena is a name on a railroad and a county landfill entrance off State Route 58 at Tower
Line Road. There is no town. Two cards were written because the depot story is genuinely good and
because the place otherwise plays as silence on a long stretch. Everything else about Bena is one
Wikipedia stub.

`near` is 35.326667, -118.739722, radius 8,000 m, from the Wikipedia article's coordinates.

  `{distance}` rather than a written-in number. That also means the card still works when it plays

## Sources found
- **Wikipedia Bena** — "Bena (formerly Pampa)"; the Pampa post office 1889-1890 and again in 1901;
  the station built in 1885; Standard Construction's four operators' houses for Southern Pacific in
  1918; the 1962 superphosphate plant on the old station site; the depot donated by Southern
  Pacific and Santa Fe to Kern Pioneer Village in 1961 and fully restored by the Kern County Museum
  in 2020; wildcat oil drilling in the 1930s and 1940s; the county landfill.
- **Kern County Museum** — "The full restoration of the Bena Railroad Depot was completed in the
  Summer of 2020. Thanks to a $20,000 grant from the Wheeler Foundation, the depot received a new
  coat of exterior paint, a new platform deck, new back porch and interior cleaning." This is an
  independent second source for the depot's survival, though not for the 1961 donation.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **"Largest superphosphate manufacturing plant west of the Mississippi River" was deliberately
  left out of the card.** That is a superlative, and §5 wants two independent sources. Wikipedia is
  the only fetched source for it. Search results say the claim traces to the US Bureau of Mines
  *Minerals Yearbook 1962* (which also gives a 50-ton-per-hour capacity and units for diammonium
  phosphate and ammonium sulfate) and that Wikipedia cites a *Fresno Bee* article of 29 April 1962,
  "Big Fertilizer Plant Starts Kern Output". Neither was fetched. The card says only "a fertilizer
  plant went up on the old station site in 1962". Get the Minerals Yearbook page and the superlative
  becomes a much better card than the one that is there now.
- **The landfill has no fetched source of its own.** The card rests on Wikipedia's bare "Kern County
  operates a landfill at the location". The official name is the Bakersfield Metropolitan (Bena)
  Sanitary Landfill, at 2951 Neumarkel Road, about 17 miles east of Bakersfield — but that came from
  search results, and both official pages failed to fetch. Do not add acreage, tonnage or the
  official name to a card until one of them is read properly.
- **The 1961 donation date and the 2020 restoration** are consistent across the two sources, but
  only Wikipedia gives 1961.
- Bena's name origin is unknown. Nothing fetched says where "Bena" came from, or why Pampa was
  dropped. The card only reports the change.
