# Cholame (town): research notes

Phase P4, writer session 2026-10-05. 2 cards written, before the §9 check.

## Where it is relative to the drive
Cholame is at 35.723889, -120.296389 (Wikipedia, GNIS 252871), on State Route 41 just southwest of
its junction with State Route 46. An 8,000 m `near` radius covers the village and the 46/41 junction.

## Cards
- persistent-name: the Salinan village behind the name, Mason's 1912 "most persistent of native
  village names", the 1804 record and the 1844 Rancho Cholame grant. Mission San Miguel is named,
  so the card carries `{distance}` and an `elsewhere` block (coordinates from the Wikipedia article
  on Mission San Miguel Arcángel).
- jack-ranch: Hollister 1867, Robert Edgar Jack 1869, the Hearst Corporation purchase, the renaming
  to honour the Jack family.
- James Dean is a sight (`subjects/ca/sight/james-dean-crash-site.md`); the fault is a landscape unit
  (`subjects/ca/landscape/san-andreas-fault.md`).

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Year of the Hearst purchase.** Hearst Ranch's own page says 1965; Wikipedia (Cholame and Hearst
  Ranch, citing Kean 1993) says 1966. The card says "since the 1960s".
- **Acreage.** Hearst Ranch: 58,000 acres when bought; Wikipedia: 73,000-acre ranch today. Prose
  figures, not a table, and they disagree in kind; not used.
- **Circle C brand.** Hearst Ranch: "second oldest registered brand in California, and the oldest
  still in use today"; Wikipedia's Hearst Ranch article says the same but cites hearstranch.com, so
  there is only one independent source for a superlative. Dropped.
- **"Largest wool grower in Central California"** (Jack) rests on Wikipedia citing a 2015 Tribune
  column. The card says "a leading wool grower".
- Mason glosses the village as possibly meaning "Tulareño village", from the Yokuts. Speculative in
  the source itself; not used.
