# Edison (town): research notes

Phase P2, writer session 2026-10-04. 3 cards written, before the §9 check.

## Where it is relative to the drive
Edison is at 35.347306, -118.869825, a few miles north of State Route 58 about seven and a half
miles east-southeast of downtown Bakersfield. The Jedediah Smith landmark is at 35.332222,
-118.806667, at Old Bena and Tower Line roads — Tower Line Road crosses State Route 58, so that
card is anchored on the landmark rather than the village and plays close to the highway.

## Sources found
- **Wikipedia Edison** — "Edison (formerly, Wade)"; "In 1902, the Edison Electric Company built a
  substation on the site"; "The Southern Pacific Railroad arrived at Wade in 1903, and changed the
  name to Edison"; post office 1903/1929/1946; oil found nearby in 1928 and the Edison Oil Field
  peaking in the 1950s; 255 people at the 2020 census, Edison's first census as a designated place;
  elevation 571 feet.
- **Wikipedia Edison Oil Field** — found July 1928 by General Petroleum Corporation; peak output in
  1953 of over 6.6 million barrels; 149 million barrels cumulative and 914 active wells at the end
  of 2008, worked by about 40 companies, the largest being Vaquero Energy with 352; oil pumps and
  storage tanks surrounded by row crops and orchards. The production figures are the article's
  citation of the California Department of Oil, Gas, and Geothermal Resources annual report, and the
  card's source carries that as its `table`.
- **CHL No. 660, Point on the Jedediah Smith Trail** — "About February 1, 1827, Jedediah Strong
  Smith, first American to reach Mexican California overland, passed near this spot with his party
  of fur trappers. From San Gabriel Mission, the group was en route north to a land reported teeming
  with 'plenty of Beaver.' Smith and his men were trailblazers whose exploits soon led to the
  American conquest of California."

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **The two landmark sources are not independent.** NoeHill and the Office of Historic Preservation
  both reproduce the same state plaque. Confidence on the Jedediah Smith card is 4 because it is an
  official inscription, not because two parties agree.
- **The landmark's location is given two ways.** The Office of Historic Preservation says the
  southeast corner of Old Bena and Tower Line Roads, 3.6 miles east of Edison; NoeHill says State
  Route 58 at Towerline Road. The card gives the road corner and says "east of Edison" without a
  number, so `{distance}` is not needed and nothing goes stale.
- **The plaque may be gone.** A landmark-tracking database reported it missing as of January 2015.
  That came from a search result, not a fetched page, so it is not in the card. Worth confirming
  before anyone tells a driver to look for a marker.
- **The last sentence of the plaque was left out** — "whose exploits soon led to the American
  conquest of California" — because it is a nineteenth-century framing of a contested event rather
  than a fact about this spot.
- The Edison name card rests on the Wikipedia article alone, so confidence is 3. Any Southern
  California Edison corporate history would make it 4; the company's own archive finding aid is at
  `oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt6199s2cp`.

## Leads not followed
- **Southern California Edison's Kern River hydro plant.** Search results say Edison Electric began
  generating at its Kern River Hydro Power House in 1907 and sent the power 118 miles to Los Angeles
  on a 75-kilovolt line, the highest voltage in the country at the time and the first on steel
  towers. That is a far better card than the substation if it can be sourced; it would probably sit
  with the Kern River or the county deck rather than Edison.
- **Magunden Substation**, established 1914 by Pacific Light and Power as part of the Big Creek
  hydroelectric project, is in Bakersfield and may be visible from the road.
- Edison was a potato-growing area in the 1940s, per the Wikipedia article's history section. One
  line, not enough for a card on its own.
- Edison's demographic make-up at the 2020 census is in the article. It would carry the
  `demographics` tag and was not used; a place of 255 people is thin ground for it.
