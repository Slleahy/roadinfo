# Paso Robles (town): research notes

Phase P5, writer session 2026-10-05. 6 cards written, before the §9 check. All anchored
`{"type": "place", "name": "Paso Robles"}` with `near` 35.628513, -120.650356 (Wikipedia), radius 8,000 m.

## The owner's "1886 hot springs" date
The plan said "the 1886 hot springs". The sources do not support that. What they say:
- Hot springs used by Indigenous people before settlement (Spanish explorers' records); called
  "California's oldest watering place" with a 1795 date attached (Wikipedia *Paso Robles Hot Springs*;
  Turkel).
- **1864**: first hotel and bathhouse at the springs (three sources agree).
- **1886**: the railroad, not the springs. First train 31 October 1886; an auction the next month
  sold 228 town lots (Wikipedia *Paso Robles*).
- **1889**: incorporation (Wikipedia gives 11 March 1889; the *Paso Robles AVA* article gives
  25 February 1889, so no day is given in the card).
- **1891**: the grand El Paso de Robles Hotel, "absolutely fireproof"; burned 1940; new Paso Robles Inn
  February 1942.
The cards use those dates.

## Cards
- pass-of-the-oaks: El Paso de Robles, Pass of the Oaks; mission-era name; 1844 Rancho Paso de Robles;
  grant covers Paso Robles, Templeton and Adelaida. The grantee's name (Pedro Narváez) left out: it
  adds nothing and is hard to say.
- oldest-watering-place: Indigenous use; "California's oldest watering place"; 1864 hotel; visitors
  from Nevada, Oregon and Idaho by 1868; the 1882 "Only Natural Mud Baths in the World" brochure.
- fireproof-hotel: 1891 hotel declared fireproof; garden, golf course, plunge; Theodore Roosevelt,
  Pirates and White Sox; destroyed by fire 1940; new inn 1942, still a hot springs resort.
- railroad-town: the 1886 train and lot auction; Blackburn Bros. & James; 1889; 1910 Southern Pacific
  brochure.
- paderewski: came 1913 for arthritis treatment; 1914 ranch west of town; Zinfandel; kept land until
  1941; Paderewski Festival.
- jesse-james-uncle: Drury James, a founder, uncle of Jesse and Frank James; 1851 cattle drive;
  the legend of the winter of 1868-69 framed as legend, with the Historical Society's own disclaimer
  that no first-hand evidence exists. Tagged `crime`.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Hotel designer**: Turkel says Jacob Leuzen; other web pages say Stanford White. Left out.
- **When the 1891 hotel was built**: Wikipedia *Paso Robles Hot Springs* says construction began in
  1889 and finished in 1900, and that "the Inn burned down" in both 1913 and 1940. Turkel and the
  *Paso Robles* article give 1891. The card follows 1891 and cites only the 1940 fire.
- Turkel says a Southern Pacific line reached Paso Robles by 1877. Wikipedia says the first train
  came in 1886. The 1886 date is used; Turkel's 1877 claim is not.
- The night clerk who died of a heart attack in the 1940 fire is left out.
- "California's oldest watering place" is a superlative. It is told as a name the town has been
  called, with two sources (Wikipedia and Turkel). Turkel may have drawn on Wikipedia.
- Travel Paso, an official tourism body, says Paderewski "planted Petite Sirah and Zinfandel". This
  came through WebFetch's summarizer, and curl was denied, so it is not cited.
- **Population**: 31,490 in 2020 according to Wikipedia. It is not in the facts file, so no card.
- **Climate**: Wikipedia gives 15.26 inches of rain at "the city's" station. The facts file's NOAA
  airport station gives 12.15. The cards use the facts file.
- Which Indigenous people lived here at contact (Northern Chumash or Salinan) is disputed in the
  sources. The card says only "the local Indigenous people".
