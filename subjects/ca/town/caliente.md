## Where it is relative to the drive
Caliente village is at 35.291111, -118.627778, about two kilometres north of State Route 58 as
the highway comes down out of Tehachapi Creek canyon. An 8,000 m `near` radius covers both the
village and the stretch of 58 below it, and also covers Bealville.

## Sources found
- **CHL No. 757 plaque** (LandmarkQuest, confirmed in outline by the Office of Historic
  Preservation listing): Allen's Camp, Gabriel Allen's cabin and stock pasture, the renaming when
  construction reached the point in April 1875, "a railroad terminal for about 16 months while a
  force of up to three thousand men, most of them Chinese, labored on the heavy railroad
  construction on the mountain."
- **ASCE** independently gives "about 3,000 Chinese laborers", picks, shovels, horse-drawn carts
  and blasting powder, 18 tunnels, ten bridges, completed 1876 in under two years. That is the
  second independent source the 3,000 figure needed.
- **Wikipedia Caliente** — the hot springs behind the name, the Telegraph Stage Line and Cerro
  Gordo Freighting Co., full-time population 200, about 60 buildings including 20 or more saloons,
  post office 1875/1883/1890, the general store remodelled in 1980 to house the post office,
  Bealville about a mile south toward SR 58 along Caliente-Bodfish Road, elevation 1,312 feet.
- **CHL No. 741 Bealville** (OHP listing) — Beale "established his home here" in 1855 and was
  Superintendent of Indian Affairs for California and Nevada. The card uses the Wikipedia wording
  for the Army and Indian Affairs roles because the OHP fetch only returned the inscription in
  fragments.
- **1932 flood** — The Loop Newspaper for the cloudburst and the debris down Tehachapi Creek
  through Keene and Caliente; usdeadlyevents quoting the state Department of Public Works for the
  death toll, the nine trestles and bridges and miles of track lost between Tehachapi and Bena,
  the boxcar carried 1¼ miles, and the two deaths at Caliente when buildings in the lower town
  were destroyed.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Dedication date of the Caliente marker.** Registered 15 February 1961; LandmarkQuest says the
  marker was dedicated 16 July 1965 and a search result said 1961. No card uses a dedication date.
- **Beale's ranch dates.** The Bealville plaque says he established his home there in 1855; the
  Wikipedia biography says he bought the Mexican land grants that became Tejon Ranch in 1865-66.
  The card uses the plaque's 1855 for the home and says nothing about the ranch purchase.
- **Camel dates.** Wikipedia gives the experiment as 1857-1859. The card says "from 1857".
- **The saloons card rests on one source.** 200 residents, 60 buildings, 20-plus saloons and the
  post office dates are all from the Wikipedia article alone, so confidence is 3. A Kern County
  Historical Society or Kern County Museum page would lift it.
- **Population 1,019.** The Wikipedia infobox carries a population of 1,019 for Caliente. It is
  not clear whether that is a CDP, a ZCTA or a historical figure, so no card uses it.

## Leads not followed
- **Caliente's Chinese dead.** Search results (not a fetched page) say more than 300 Chinese
  workers were buried in a basin east of Caliente and later exhumed and returned to China. That is
  a strong, sober card if two fetchable sources can be found. The Courthouse News article is the
  obvious one and it returned 403 to this fetcher; try a different fetcher or the Chinese Railroad
  Workers in North America Project at Stanford.
- **Caliente Creek floods since 1932.** The creek has flooded repeatedly; the 1932 event is the one
  with sources. Nothing fetched on later floods.
- **Hart Flat**, up the canyon toward Keene, was flagged in the Keene notes as P2 material. Nothing
  was fetched on it this session and it has no card. Desert News ran a joint history of Keene and
  Hart Flat.
- **Bealville as its own subject.** CHL 741 sits on Bealville Road 0.9 miles north of SR 58, close
  enough to the highway to be a sight in its own right if the Beale story is worth more than the
  one card it has.
- The **Caliente General Store and post office** is a thing a driver can actually stop at. Worth a
  card of its own if a local source describes it.
