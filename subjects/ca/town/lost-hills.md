# Lost Hills (town): research notes

Phase P3, writer session 2026-10-04. 3 cards written (cap 8). Before the §9 check.

## Where it plays
`near` is 35.61639, -119.69417, radius 8,000 m, from the Wikipedia infobox. The town sits right at
the Interstate 5 exit for Highway 46, so the standard town radius is enough.

## Cards
- `named-for-the-hills` — the Lost Hills Range, the post office opening and closing and opening,
  population.
- `wonderful-pistachios` — the Wonderful Company as the town's major employer, its share of the
  national pistachio crop, Paramount Farms building a park and houses here, the Kern Water Bank.
- `green-bridge` — the lizard-shaped footbridge over Highway 46. Tagged `tragedy`: it was built
  after a pedestrian was killed crossing.

## Fetch tally
5 of 12: Wikipedia Lost Hills, California (twice — second pass for the lead and the bridge sentences
word for word); Wikipedia The Wonderful Company; de-simone.com (301 to the firm's home page, not
followed); the Wonderful Company's own corporate responsibility article on the bridge.

## Sources found
- Wikipedia Lost Hills — the Lost Hills Range, the 1911/1912/1913/1937 post office, 2,370 people at
  the 2020 census down from 2,412, 305 feet, the Wonderful Company as a major employer, Paramount
  Farms building a park and housing in the early 2000s, and the bridge in full.
- Wikipedia The Wonderful Company — Stewart and Lynda Resnick, Paramount Farms formed 1989,
  15% to 20% of the United States pistachio crop as of 2024 mostly exported to China, revenue over
  $4 billion, 9,000 employees, 57% of the Kern Water Bank, which holds a million acre-feet.
- `csr.wonderful.com` — residents raised the danger with government officials and were told there
  was no money for a bridge. This is the funder's own page, so it is used only for what the company
  did and said, and the card does not repeat its language about the design.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **The hills' size.** A first pass read the article as "the low Lost Hills Range, which extends
  approximately ten miles". The verbatim sentence has no length in it. The ten miles is left out.
- **"Sixth largest by remaining reserves in California."** That phrase is in both the Lost Hills
  town article and the Lost Hills Oil Field article. Two Wikipedia pages are not two independent
  sources, and it is a ranking claim, so §5 says drop it. Dropped from every card. CalGEM's annual
  report would settle it.
- **The bridge's dates and dimensions.** Search results give a 176-foot span, 10-foot width and
  309-foot spiral ramps, and disagree about whether it opened in 2023 or 2024. None of that is in a
  fetched source, so none of it is in the card. The engineer's page (DeSimone) 301-redirects to the
  firm's home page.

## Leads not followed
- The Wonderful Company's other investments in the town — a health clinic, a school called Wonderful
  Academy, soccer fields. Worth one more card if a non-company source can be found.
- Lost Hills was 98.02% Hispanic or Latino at the 2020 census, with median household income $42,333
  in 2023. A `demographics`-tagged card is defensible under §7 but wants the ACS table, not the
  Wikipedia prose.
- Highway 46's nickname, "Blood Alley", appears in the Wonderful article tied to James Dean. That
  belongs to phase P4 (Cholame), not here, and was deliberately left out.
