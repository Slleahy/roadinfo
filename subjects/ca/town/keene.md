## Sources found
- `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keene,_California` — founding as Wells in 1876, the two namesakes,
  the post office dates, 2020 population 469 and 2010 population 431, elevation 2,602 feet.
- `https://www.desertnews.com/news/article_3d23808e-2096-11ec-92b8-0f053d2bfe08.html` — independent
  confirmation of the names and the post office, plus the National Chavez Center opening in 2004.
- `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Kern_County_earthquake` — two tunnels collapsed between
  Tehachapi and Marcel, six more damaged, maximum intensity near Bealville, line reopened in 25 days.
- `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodford,_California` — redirects to Keene; the only content is the
  one sentence about Woodford's 30 buildings a mile southeast.

## Open questions
- Keene is a census-designated place, so it has no incorporation history and no city government to
  cite. Nothing in the deck needs one.
- Marcel and Bealville are both named in the earthquake source but neither is placed. If a later
  phase can source their positions, the quake card could say which side of Keene the tunnels were on.
- The Tehachapi Loop overlook is on Woodford-Tehachapi Road out of Keene, which is how most
  visitors see the Loop, but the only support found was a railfan site. Worth one fetch of a Kern
  County or Caltrans page in a later phase; it would make a strong fourth Keene card.

## Leads not followed
- Keene's school, store and hotel: the Desert News history did not cover them, and nothing else was
  fetched. Local-history material almost certainly exists.
- Hart Flat, covered in the same Desert News article, is on the far side of the pass and belongs to
  the P2 Caliente batch rather than here.
- Helper engines: trains are still helped over the Tehachapi grade, and Keene is on the climb. No
  source fetched this session says so plainly. Good stretch-card material for P7.

## Checking pass, 2026-10-04
- **Woodford-Tehachapi Road was cut.** The card said Woodford's "name survives on
  Woodford-Tehachapi Road". The only support is the Keene article giving Chavez's burial address as
  29700 Woodford Tehachapi Rd — that places the UFW headquarters on the road, but says nothing about
  where the road's name came from. Inference, so it came out. Still a good card if a Kern County
  road-naming source ever turns up.
- "About a mile southeast of Keene" was reworded to "just outside Keene": the listener is standing
  in Keene when this plays, so a written-in distance is exactly what `{distance}` exists to replace,
  and Woodford has no citable coordinates to measure to.
- The `Woodford, California` URL redirects to the Keene article, so it was never a second source.
  Merged into the Keene citation.
- **The 25-days card got better, not worse.** The full Wikipedia sentences were re-read: two tunnels
  collapsed, six more damaged, 18-inch reinforced concrete walls cracked, the gap between one
  tunnel's entrances reduced by up to 8 ft 2 in, and the line reopened in 25 days "by a construction
  force of one thousand men and 175 pieces of earth-moving equipment". Bealville was dropped from
  the card (it is a place off the road with no distance given, and the damage detail carries itself)
  and the thousand men were put in.
