# The Bakersfield Sound (sight): research notes

Phase P2, writer session 2026-10-04. 3 cards written, the sight cap. Before the §9 check.

## Why a sight and not part of the town deck
The sound is the one thing most listeners already associate with Bakersfield, and it has enough in
it for three cards. Putting it in the town deck would have eaten most of the town's eight slots and
pushed out the founding, the fire, the quake, the river and the oil. It is filed as a sight,
anchored on Buck Owens Boulevard where the Crystal Palace stands, which is the only place in the
city where the sound is a thing you can point at.

`anchor` and `near` are 35.386, -119.043 with a 10,000 m reach, from the Wikipedia coordinates for
the Crystal Palace. That is wider than the usual 5,000 m for a sight, because the subject is the
whole city's music scene and not one building.

## Sources found
- **Wikipedia Bakersfield sound** — the Dust Bowl audience from Oklahoma, Texas, Arkansas and the
  Midwest; the music taking shape "at honky-tonk bars such as The Blackboard, and on local
  television stations in Bakersfield and throughout California in the 1950s and 1960s"; "twin Fender
  Telecasters with a picking style (as opposed to strumming), a big drum beat, and fiddle, with an
  occasional prominent pedal steel guitar solo"; "a reaction against the slickly produced,
  orchestra-laden Nashville sound"; "the major radio station in Nashville, WSM, did not reach
  California"; Bill Woods on piano on Bud Hobbs's 1954 "Louisiana Swing" with Owens on lead guitar;
  Wynn Stewart credited with pioneering the sound; the Crystal Palace opening in 1996.
- **Wikipedia Buck Owens Crystal Palace** — 2800 Buck Owens Boulevard, 35.386, -119.043; opened 23
  October 1996; the Buck Owens Museum in cases around the first floor; Western Revival style;
  **closed in August 2025** after about 28 years.
- **Alta Journal** — Owens bought his first Telecaster in 1951, the year it came out; its "jangly
  treble" could "cut through the racket of booming bass drums", against Nashville's "warmer
  hollow-body electrics" and "syrupy strings"; Haggard's family settled in Oildale "a little over
  three miles north" of the honky-tonks; Owens moved to Bakersfield in May 1951; both men recorded
  at Capitol in Hollywood.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **The Telecaster card rests on Alta alone**, so confidence is 3. It is the best card of the three
  to listen to, and a second source would be worth one fetch: a Fender history, a Country Music Hall
  of Fame page, or the Ken Burns *Country Music* companion material.
- **The Crystal Palace is closed.** The card says so, which matters: an open card would send a
  driver to a shut building. Only Wikipedia reports the August 2025 closure, and it is recent enough
  that it should be re-checked before release.
- **Owens's arrival date differs by source.** Wikipedia says he and his first wife settled in
  Bakersfield in 1951; Alta says May 1951. The cards say 1951.
- **The Blackboard** is named but not placed. No fetched source gives its address or whether the
  building survives. A card about standing where The Blackboard was would be better than the one
  about the Crystal Palace, if it can be sourced and if the site is near the road.
- The NARM page was a dud. Do not spend another fetch on it.

## Leads not followed
- **Bill Woods.** Named in the Wikipedia article as the man on piano on the 1954 session, and
  described elsewhere as the organiser of the whole scene. If his own article says he worked in
  Bakersfield, he is a plausible person card — but see the notability bar discussion in
  `subjects/ca/person/bakersfield-people.md`.
- **Wynn Stewart**, credited with pioneering the sound. Same test.
- **The Kern County Museum's Bakersfield Sound exhibit.** Over 100 artists, original signage and
  instruments, and the Haggard boxcar house. The museum's own exhibit page was not fetched; the
  NARM blurb was all that was read. A card about the exhibit, with `{distance}` to 35.393, -119.020,
  would give the music deck a place a driver can actually go now that the Crystal Palace is shut.
- **Red Simpson, Bonnie Owens and Fuzzy Owen** are honoured in the museum exhibit. Trucker songs
  (Red Simpson) are tempting for a road narrator but he is below the recognition bar.
- **Local television.** The Wikipedia article says the sound spread on local television stations.
  *Cousin Herb Henson's Trading Post* on KERO is the show usually named. Not fetched, not written.
- **Capitol Records in Hollywood**, where both men recorded, is off this corridor entirely and does
  not belong in a Bakersfield card.

## Names a voice may get wrong
Telecaster, honky-tonk and Buckaroos should be fine. "WSM" was deliberately not written as letters:
the card says "Nashville's big radio station" instead, because a voice reading call letters aloud is
a risk for no gain. No `pronunciations` field was added.
