# James Dean crash site and memorial (sight): research notes

Phase P4, writer session 2026-10-05. 3 cards written (the sight cap), before the §9 check.
All three are tagged `tragedy`. Per PLAYBOOK §3a Dean is not a `person` card: dying here is not a tie.

## Where it is
- Crash site: 35.734444, -120.284444, the junction of SR 46 and SR 41 (Wikipedia, *Death of James
  Dean*, infobox and map). `near` radius 5,000 m.
- Memorial: in front of the former Cholame post office near the Jack Ranch Cafe. No separate
  coordinates were fetched (HMDB 403), so the memorial card uses the Cholame village coordinates
  with a 5,000 m radius. ENR puts the memorial about half a mile from the interchange.

## Cards
- last-drive: date, age 24, the Porsche Spyder bound for Salinas, coming west down the Antelope Grade,
  the left-turning Ford, almost head-on; dead on arrival; passenger survived.
- the-wye: James Dean Memorial Junction (2005), "the Wye" built 1958 with a higher than average
  collision rate, Blood Alley, the 2025 overpass that ends the left turn.
- memorial: 1977, Seita Ohnishi of Kobe, stainless steel around a tree of heaven, designed in Japan,
  the Little Prince line.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Overpass opening.** KEYT (11 June 2025) says traffic would start "as soon as this week"; the
  Wikipedia SR 46 article still says completion "scheduled ... in 2026"; a Gribblenation search
  result said it opened in June 2025. The card says "due to open in June 2025" to stay inside the
  fetched source. A checker with a Caltrans page could firm this up.
- **Speed.** Wikipedia reports both "approximately 85 mph" claims and the CHP corporal's estimate of
  about 55 mph. Contested; left out.
- **Cost figures** ($171M approved, $147M overall, $13,000 for the memorial) are prose, not tables;
  left out.
- **Memorial date.** One search snippet said made in Japan in 1977 and placed in 1978. Both
  Wikipedia articles say erected 1977. Card says "put up in 1977"; a checker may prefer to hedge.
- Donald Turnupseed is not named: a private person, not needed for the story.
