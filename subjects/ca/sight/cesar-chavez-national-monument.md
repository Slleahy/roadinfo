# Cesar E. Chavez National Monument / Nuestra Senora Reina de la Paz (sight): research notes

Phase P1, writer session 2026-10-04. 3 cards written, the sight cap. Before the §9 check.

## Cards
- sanatorium-first, chavez-home, designation.
- `near` is 35.227222, -118.561389, radius 5,000 m, from the Wikipedia article's coordinates. The
  site is on Woodford-Tehachapi Road just off State Route 58 at Keene.
- No `person` card was written. Chavez was born in Yuma, Arizona and the union was founded in
  Delano; under §3a the connection to this place is "did the work here and is buried here", which
  the sight cards carry better than a biography card would.

## Sources found
- `https://www.nps.gov/articles/chavez.htm` — the strongest single source: the 1913 Kern County
  quarry, the state tuberculosis bureau and Stony Brook Retreat, the 1970 tip about the 187-acre
  property going to auction, the headquarters becoming official in January 1972, the lettuce
  boycott and the California Agricultural Labor Relations Act of 1975, Chavez's death in April
  1993 and Helen Chavez burying him there, and the October 8 2012 proclamation.
- `https://www.nps.gov/places/000/ch-vez-home.htm` — the family moved in in 1971; eight children;
  "The house's modesty and simplicity depict the values of the Chavez family."
- `https://www.nps.gov/places/000/dormitory-and-former-tuberculosis-sanatorium.htm` — patients from
  the 1930s until it closed in 1967; turned over in 1970.
- Wikipedia — 116 acres, the 398th unit of the National Park System, co-managed with the National
  Chavez Center, the visitor centre and memorial garden.

## Deliberately left out
- **The movie producer.** Search results name Ed Lewis as the benefactor who bought the property at
  auction without revealing his tie to the union, then leased and sold it on. The National Park
  Service page fetch returned this as a paraphrase, not as a quotable sentence, so the card says
  only that the property "was turned over to Cesar Chavez and the farm worker movement" in 1970.
  This is the best unused lead in the deck: it is a good story and it is almost certainly sourceable
  from the National Historic Landmark nomination (see below).
- **The 1971 assassination plot.** Federal agents warned Chavez of a plot and he left La Paz for a
  time. Search results attribute this to National Park Service pages, but no fetched page carried
  it. Sensitive and needs a solid source; would be tagged `crime`.
- Helen Chavez spent a year at the site as a child when it was still a sanatorium, and did not want
  to move back. A memorable detail, again only in a search summary.
- The dates do not quite agree on when the headquarters moved: Chavez relocated in spring 1971, the
  site "officially became the UFW headquarters in January 1972", and the 2012 proclamation says
  1972. The cards use 1971 for the family moving in and January 1972 for the headquarters, which is
  consistent with both.

## Words the narrator will guess at
Nuestra Señora Reina de la Paz is written into the designation card with its Spanish spelling,
because it is the place's own name and appears on the site. No pronunciation was invented. This one
should go to the owner, along with Chavez.
