## Sources found
- `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehachapi_Loop` — the circle's dimensions, the 77 feet gained, the
  3,800-foot train that passes over itself, the 3,000 Chinese workers between 1875 and 1876,
  William Hood, Walong siding, BNSF 20 trains a day and Union Pacific 16.
- `https://www.asce.org/.../tehachapi-pass-railroad-line` — independent confirmation of the
  3,000 Chinese labourers and their tools, 18 tunnels and ten bridges, built in under two years,
  an average of 36 freight trains a day, and the loop's 1,210-foot diameter.
- `https://ohp.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=21423` — landmark 508: "the world-renowned Loop completed in
  1876 under the direction of William Hood, Southern Pacific railroad engineer."

## Conflicts noted for the checker
- Loop length: Wikipedia 3,779 feet, ASCE 3,799 feet. The cards avoid the figure entirely and use
  the diameter, which both sources agree on at about 1,210 feet.
- Grade: Wikipedia "a steady two-percent grade", ASCE "averages 2.2% in gradient across its
  28-mile length". The card says "a steady grade of around two percent", true on both readings.
- Trains per day: ASCE 36; Wikipedia 20 plus 16, which also gives 36; American-Rails 30 to 40;
  Tehachapi News about 50. The deck uses 36.

  Left out as speculative, but it is a live story worth a card once construction is decided.

## Checking pass, 2026-10-04
- **Thirty-six trains a day: keep it.** Two sources land there independently. Wikipedia gives
  "BNSF operates about 20 trains through the Loop per day, while Union Pacific sends about 16 trains
  daily" (as of 2025), and the ASCE landmark page says the line "accommodates an average of 36
  freight trains per day". American-Rails' 30-40 brackets that, and Tehachapi News' "about 50" is
  the outlier. Both cards hedge it ("about", "an average of around"), which is the honest way to say
  a figure that moves with traffic. No change.
- Everything else in the Loop deck is verbatim: 3,779 feet of spiral, the 1,210-foot circle, 77 feet
  gained at a steady two percent, trains over 3,800 feet (about 56 boxcars) passing over themselves,
  Walong after district roadmaster W. A. Long, about 3,000 Chinese workers between 1875 and 1876
  with hand tools and blasting powder through solid and decomposed granite, 18 tunnels and ten
  bridges, under two years, William Hood. The Loop's coordinates (35.200833, -118.536944) match the
  Wikipedia infobox exactly.
