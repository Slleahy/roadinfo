# Tule Elk State Natural Reserve (sight): research notes

Phase P3, writer session 2026-10-04. 1 card (cap 3). Before the §9 check.

## Why it is in this batch
It was not on the assigned list, but it is on the ground the driver crosses: the reserve is a few
miles south of the Bakersfield-to-Interstate-5 run, and the man who saved the elk is the same
Henry Miller whose headquarters stood at Buttonwillow. That gives the card a legitimate local
opening under §4 instead of a fact about somewhere else.

- `near`: 35.40056, -119.46944 (Buttonwillow), radius 20,000 m.
- `elsewhere`: 35.3214, -119.3642, the reserve, from the Wikipedia infobox.

## Sources found
- California State Parks — the elk believed extinct when hunting was banned in 1873, an 1895 count
  finding only 28 animals, Miller providing 600 acres of open range and rewarding workers who
  reported disturbances, a 1874 tip leading game warden A. C. Tibbets to survivors, the state
  buying 953 acres near Tupman in 1932, an ideal herd of 30 to 35, surplus animals moved to places
  including Carrizo Plain National Monument.
- Wikipedia — the population collapsing "to double digits by the late 1800s", Miller sheltering the
  survivors in the wetlands around Buena Vista Lake, the Tupman Zoological Reserve established 1932
  with about 175 elk from the Miller and Lux herd, state management from 1953, about 6,000 tule elk
  in California as of 2023, coordinates 35.3214, -119.3642.

## Conflicts
- **Reserve size.** State Parks says 953 acres, Wikipedia says 960. The card uses 953 on the state's
  own authority.
- **How many elk started the reserve.** Wikipedia says about 175 came from the Miller and Lux herd
  in 1932 and that Miller's protected herd had grown to about 400 head by 1914. State Parks does not
  give either figure. Neither is in the card.
- **The 28 animals.** State Parks gives the 1895 count; Wikipedia independently says the population
  fell to double digits in the late 1800s. That is two sources agreeing in substance, which is what
  §5 wants for a number this startling.

## Leads not followed
- A. C. Tibbets and the 1874 tip — a named-person story that would make the card more vivid, but it
  needs its own sentence and the card is already at four.
- Buena Vista Lake itself: the other great vanished lake of the southern valley, drained like Tulare
  Lake. It is close to the route and has no card. Strong candidate for the landscape deck.
- The 6,000 tule elk in California as of 2023, and the transplants to Carrizo Plain — a recovery
  card, and Carrizo Plain is near the phase P4 ground on Highway 46.
