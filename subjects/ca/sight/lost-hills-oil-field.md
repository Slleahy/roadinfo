Phase P3, writer session 2026-10-04. 3 cards, the sight cap. Fetch budget fully spent. Before the
§9 check.

## Why it is on the road and not off it
Highway 46 runs through the field from east to west, and Interstate 5 crosses its eastern end, so
no `elsewhere` block is needed: the driver is in it. `near` and the anchor are 35.615886,
-119.724706, from the Wikipedia article's coordinates, radius 12,000 m because the field is long
and the pumpjacks are visible well before and after.

## Cards

- `diatomite` — what the reservoir rock is, 70% empty space, 2.2 billion barrels in place, five
  percent recovered, fracturing.

## Sources found
- Wikipedia Lost Hills Oil Field — Martin and Dudley, July 1910, the Etchegoin Pool at 530 feet;
  the 1917 peak of 4 million barrels and 12.2 million in 2007; the Belridge Diatomite of the
  Monterey Formation, 45% to 70% porosity, almost 50% saturated, three to four percent recovered
  from that unit; Chevron's 2.2 billion barrels in place with 5% extracted; subsidence up to 8 feet
  centrally and about 9 inches a year field-wide; Chevron and Aera as operators in 2008; Highway 46
  crossing the field and the town sitting on the far side of the aqueduct.
- NASA JPL PIA03458 — parts of the field subsiding more than 3 centimetres, 1.2 inches, a month in
  1995 and 1996, measured with the European Space Agency's ERS-1 and ERS-2 radar satellites; the
  field about 1.5 km wide and 6 km long, 60 km northwest of Bakersfield.
- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — diatomite "made of countless silica skeletons of
  microscopic aquatic plants", up to 70 percent empty space, light enough to float on water, nearly
  impermeable, less than ten percent recoverable, and fracturing as the only way to produce it.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **The size ranking is not in any card.** "18th largest in California by size, sixth by remaining
  reserves, over 110 million barrels" comes from one Wikipedia article and is echoed by another.
  §5 needs two independent sources for a ranking. CalGEM's Annual Report of the State Oil and Gas
  Supervisor (2023 edition posted November 2025) is the source that would settle it, and it would
  also give a current production figure. The newest production number in hand is 12.2 million
  barrels in 2007, which is too stale to put in a card, so no production card covers today.
- **Distance from Bakersfield** is given inconsistently by JPL itself: 100 km on the subsidence
  animation page, 60 km on the interferogram page. Neither is in a card.
- The JPL interferogram page does not name Fielding or Patzek; the Wiley abstract that would have
  is behind a 403.

## Leads not followed
- NASA JPL PIA13204, the Lost Hills subsidence animation: 1982 to 2000, about 3 metres of maximum
  subsidence, from a USGS map and the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission. A second, independent
  figure for the total drop, and worth a fetch next time.
- Waterflooding from 1992 onward to slow the subsidence, and the damage subsidence does to well
  casings. Good material, needs an operator or agency source.
- Produced-water disposal and groundwater salinity in the Lost Hills and Belridge fields (an AAPG
  *Environmental Geosciences* paper). Would carry no `sensitive` tag but needs careful, neutral
  handling.
