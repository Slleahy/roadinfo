# Monolith (sight): research notes

Phase P1, writer session 2026-10-04. 3 cards written, the sight cap. Before the §9 check.

## Why a sight and not a town
Monolith has no census population, no post office of its own any more and no houses: the company
removed the town in the 1960s and 1970s. What is left beside State Route 58 is the cement plant,
the quarry scar on the hill and a substation. It is a thing you see, not a place you are in, so it
is filed as a sight.

## Sources found
- `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolith,_California` — the camp for aqueduct cement workers, the
  Aqueduct post office opening in 1908 and becoming Monolith in 1910, William Mulholland naming it
  for the limestone deposit, and the 2014 battery at Monolith Substation.
- Tehachapi News — the townsite dating to about 1908, the City of Los Angeles using the plant for
  the aqueduct and other Department of Water and Power work, investors buying it in the early
  1920s, houses and a bunkhouse across the road, as many as 350 residents, the company deciding in
  the 1960s that it did not want to own a town, the store closing in 1971 and the houses coming down.
- The Loop Newspaper — 900,000 barrels of cement going to the aqueduct, the narrow-gauge railway
  carrying lime ore across the valley, the 1912 quarry on Jameson Mountain, and the plant becoming
  Calaveras and then Lehigh Southwest Cement.
- `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehachapi_Energy_Storage_Project` — 8 MW / 32 MWh, 608,832
  lithium-ion cells, Southern California Edison, commissioned 2014.

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Dates for the plant.** One account has it completed in 1908 and shut in 1914 when the aqueduct
  was done; a historical marker says the City of Los Angeles began production in January 1909 near
  White Rock Creek. The cards avoid a completion date and use only the 1908 post office and the
  1910 renaming, which two sources agree on.
- **Current owner.** Search results give a chain of Monolith Portland Cement (1921), a sale in
  1989, Calaveras, Lehigh Southwest, UNACEM, and Tehachapi Cement LLC in 2023. The two fetched
  sources only reach Lehigh Southwest, so no card names a current owner. A card saying who runs the
  plant today would need the plant's own page or the UNACEM environmental product declaration at
  `astm.org` (a PDF, seen in search results, not fetched).
- **"Largest lithium-ion battery system in North America"** rests on two Wikipedia pages, which is
  not two independent sources. Confidence set to 3. An independent source (a Southern California
  Edison or Department of Energy release from 2014) would fix this, or the claim can be dropped and
  the card still works on the 608,832 cells alone.

## Leads not followed
- The Historical Marker Database has two relevant markers, `hmdb.org/m.asp?m=52988` (Monolith) and
  `m=134506` (Cement Production in Tehachapi). Both returned HTTP 403 this session. Marker texts are
  good official sources and are worth retrying with a different fetcher.
- Segregated housing at the company town: search results say Mexican and Indian families lived north
  of the railroad and Caucasian families south. That is a real and tellable fact, it would carry the
  `demographics` tag, and it needs a properly fetched source before it goes in a card.
- The narrow-gauge railway that carried lime ore diagonally across the valley, and the quarry scar
  still visible on the hill, would make a good fourth card if the sight cap were raised.

## Checking pass, 2026-10-04
- **The battery is gone.** The card was written in the present tense ("It holds 32 megawatt-hours
  and can deliver 8 megawatts"), but the Tehachapi Energy Storage Project page says it "operated
  successfully for eight years before formal decommissioning in 2022". Rewritten throughout in the
  past tense, with the eight-year life and the 2022 shutdown added, plus the sourced detail that it
  could power 1,600 to 2,400 homes for four hours. `asOf` moved from 2014 to 2022.
- **The barrels number held up and got better.** The Loop Newspaper says "Of the 1,200,000 barrels,
  900,000 barrels went to the aqueduct project", in the plant's first two and a half years from
  1909. The card now gives both figures. Still only one source for it; the Tehachapi News article
  carries no production figure at all.
- **A citation that supported nothing.** On the company-town card, the second source was quoted only
  for "the cement plant changed hands and became Calaveras and then Lehigh Southwest Cement Co." —
  a fact that is nowhere in the card. Dropped. The whole card now rests on the Tehachapi News
  article, which does carry every sentence verbatim, including the 350 residents, the bunkhouse, the
  1960s decision and Ed Tompkins closing the store in 1971.
- Monolith's coordinates (35.12, -118.374167) match the Wikipedia infobox exactly.
