# California Aqueduct (sight): research notes

Phase P3, writer session 2026-10-04. 2 cards (cap 3). Before the §9 check.

## Filed as a sight, not a landscape unit
It is a built thing the driver can see out of the window for sixty miles, so it is a sight. That
costs it the landscape unit's 25-fetch budget, which is the right trade here: the material that
matters to a driver is the canal itself and the pumping plant, and both are well covered.

## Where the cards play
The aqueduct is linear and has no single coordinate, so each card borrows a cited point on the
route and widens the radius:

- `canal-beside-the-road` — 35.61639, -119.69417 (Lost Hills), radius 20,000 m.
- `over-the-tehachapis` — 35.40056, -119.46944 (Buttonwillow), radius 20,000 m, with `elsewhere`
  at the Edmonston Pumping Plant, 34.942361, -118.824664.

Spacing them 20 miles apart keeps the program director from playing both in the same few minutes.

## Sources found
- Wikipedia California Aqueduct — 444 miles total, 13,100 cubic feet a second maximum, construction
  from 1963, officially the Governor Edmund G. Brown California Aqueduct, the principal feature of
  the State Water Project serving 35 million people and 5.7 million acres of farmland, a typical
  section 40 feet at the base with about 30 feet of water, pumped 1,926 feet over the Tehachapis at
  Edmonston.
- Wikipedia Edmonston Pumping Plant — 1,926 feet, fourteen four-stage 80,000-horsepower centrifugal
  pumps, up to 787 MW on a dedicated 230 kV line, "the most powerful water-lifting system in the
  world, other than pumped-storage hydroelectricity stations", at 34.942361, -118.824664.
- Wikipedia Interstate 5 in California — the Path 15 transmission corridor following the highway
  "forming an infrastructure corridor along with the California Aqueduct"; the West Side Freeway
  from Wheeler Ridge to Woodland; phases completed 1967, 1972 and 1979; the route chosen over the
  Golden State Highway alignment precisely because it bypasses every valley town.

## What was dropped and why
- **"The most powerful water-lifting system in the world."** One source only, and it is a
  superlative, so §5 drops it. A Department of Water Resources or Bureau of Reclamation page would
  restore it, and it is the best line available about this plant.
- **Subsidence eating the canal's capacity.** The Department of Water Resources page 403s. The
  figure went into the valley-floor landscape deck instead, on the U.S. Geological Survey's wording
  about reduced freeboard and flow capacity, which is a cleaner source anyway. If a DWR page can be
  fetched, the 2023 Delivery Capability Report addendum reportedly puts the capacity loss at 44%,
  which would make a third aqueduct card on its own.
- The aqueduct's flow direction is nowhere stated plainly in the fetched text, so no card says the
  water is heading south. The Edmonston lift carries the idea without asserting it.

## Leads not followed
