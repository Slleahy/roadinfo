# Santa Lucia Range (landscape unit): research notes

Phase P5, writer session 2026-10-05. 3 cards, before the §9 check. Unit id `santa-lucia-range`.
All cards carry `near` at Adelaida (35.64555556, -120.87361111, Wikipedia), radius 20,000 m. That
covers the drive from downtown Paso Robles west to Adelaida, where the range is ahead, and keeps
the cards from playing on the Big Sur coast.

## Cards
- saint-lucy: the range west of Paso Robles, with Adelaida in it; Carmel to San Luis Obispo County;
  named by Vizcaíno in 1602 for Saint Lucy; a barrier to explorers from the sea; Portolá in 1769
  turned inland.
- rain-shadow: wet west slopes with redwood and Douglas fir; dry east side with chaparral, pine and oak;
  12.15 inches a year at the Paso Robles airport (NOAA, facts file); about 25 inches in the Adelaida
  District (TTB table).
- granite-on-the-move: the Salinian Block's granite lies west of the San Andreas and moves northwest;
  named for the Salinas Valley; slivers carried northwest over about 30 million years; its origin
  is debated, Sierra Nevada or Mojave. The USGS 1976 abstract calls it the "ripped-off" Salinian
  block. Follows on to the San Andreas deck.

## Not used, and why
- "Steepest coastal slope in the contiguous United States" and Cone Peak as "the highest peak in
  proximity to the ocean": superlatives with one source, and on the Big Sur coast, far from the route.
- "Largest population of mountain lions in the United States": single-source superlative.
- The southernmost redwoods and the 225 and 90 range-limit species: one source, and far away.
- The Dark Watchers folklore: no reliable source fetched that reports the legend.
- The 1987 PSA Flight 1771 crash near Cayucos: off route.
- Rainfall "16 to 60 inches" across the range: Wikipedia prose; not a table.
- The card names Portolá but does not say the expedition was the "first" land exploration, a
  single-source first.
