# Phase P1 addition, writer session 2026-10-04

## Fetch tally for P1

## Sources found
- `https://scedc.caltech.edu/earthquake/kern1952.html` — the White Wolf fault described as "a
  reverse fault (with some left-lateral component of slip)", 12 lives, and "the largest earthquake
  in southern California since the Fort Tejon earthquake of 1857 and the Owens Valley earthquake of
  1872".
- `https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70021492` — "the 1952 MW (moment magnitude) 7.3 Kern County
  earthquake", the authoritative second source for the magnitude.
- `https://ohp.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=21423` — landmark 97, Oak Creek Pass: Garces in 1776, Fremont
  in 1844 to 1845, and Oak Creek Pass as "the only route used through the Tehachapi Mountains"
  until 1876. Also landmarks 508 (the Loop), 643 (Old Town) and 1056 (La Paz), used by other decks.

## Magnitude: settled, with a caveat
Published estimates for the 1952 earthquake range from Mw 7.2 to 7.5. Wikipedia and USGS
publication 70021492 both say 7.3, so the card states 7.3 with both cited. The Caltech page says
Mw 7.5 and a 2018 reanalysis preferred an intensity magnitude of 7.2. A checker should know this is
a real disagreement in the literature, not an error in the card.

## Leads still not followed
- The 90 km fault length is Wikipedia's; the card renders it as "about 55 miles". A USGS Quaternary
  Fault and Fold Database entry would give a better figure and a mapped trace.
- Whether the 1952 earthquake was induced by oil production at Wheeler Ridge: there is a USGS
  publication asking exactly that ("Was the Mw 7.5 1952 Kern County, California, earthquake induced
  (or triggered)?"). That is an excellent card if the paper supports it clearly. Not fetched.
- "Birth of a fault" describes a previously unrecognised blind strike-slip fault forming between the
  1952 and 1946 Walker Pass earthquakes, across the southern Sierra Nevada. A new fault being born
  is a strong card; only the abstract was read, and it fits the southern Sierra Nevada unit as much
  as this one.
- Peak elevations are still unresolved (see the R6 notes above). No card names a summit.
- California condor: still Wikipedia-only, still unused.

## Checking pass, 2026-10-04
- **Magnitude 7.3 stands.** Re-opened both sources: the Wikipedia infobox gives `Mw 7.3, Ms 7.5`,
  and USGS publication 70021492's abstract says "the 1952 MW (moment magnitude) 7.3 Kern County
  earthquake" word for word. Caltech's 7.5 is the older surface-wave figure for the same event, not
  a competing moment magnitude. Two independent sources on 7.3, so the card states it plainly.
- **"Crossed by" was wrong.** The Wikipedia White Wolf Fault article puts the fault "along the
  northwestern transition of the Tejon Hills and Tehachapi Mountains with the San Joaquin Valley" —
  it runs along the range's edge, it does not cross it. The card's opening sentence was rewritten
  and that article added as a fourth source.
- **The 55-mile length was dropped.** The card cited Wikipedia (90 km / 56 mi) and Caltech for the
  same clause, but Caltech says the fault "is traceable for only about 48 km (34 miles)". Two cited
  sources, two different numbers, so the clause came out. The USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold
  Database entry is still the right place to settle it.
- **Oak Creek Pass card.** Landmark 97's full text was re-fetched and the quote is exact, including
  the "Until the railroad…" sentence. The card had that fact twice, in sentences one and three, so
  the third came out. The card was also missing `near` and was naming a place off the road with no
  distance: it now anchors on Tehachapi Pass (35.10222, -118.28278, from the Wikipedia article) and
  carries `{distance}` to Oak Creek Pass (35.061, -118.389, from its Wikipedia article, about six
  miles). Note the OHP page's own location line for landmark 97 reads "74 mi SE of Tehachapi",
  which is plainly wrong; the Wikipedia coordinates were used instead.
