# San Andreas Fault (landscape unit): research notes

Phase P4, writer session 2026-10-05. 4 cards written, before the §9 check. Unit id
`san-andreas-fault`; this is the first deck on the fault. All four cards carry `near` at Cholame
(35.723889, -120.296389, radius 15,000 m), because each is about the fault where CA-46 crosses it.

## Cards
- trenches-at-cholame: the highway crosses the fault; the 1966 earthquake offset the SR 46 pavement
  about 4 inches; Caltrans's two 300-foot trenches for the new bridge; the "James Dean
  Paleoseismic Site" (tagged `tragedy` for the passing mention of the crash).
- locked-south-of-cholame: plate boundary (about 800 miles), creeping section to the northwest,
  locked south of Cholame, where 1857 struck.
- 1857: Fort Tejon earthquake, magnitude 7.9, epicentre near Cholame, rupture about 160 miles
  southeast, up to 30 feet of slip; the trench also shows the Great Flood of 1861.
- parkfield: the regular magnitude-6 earthquakes (1857, 1881, 1901, 1922, 1934, 1966), the 1988–1993
  forecast, the instruments, 2004; the motto. Parkfield is off route, so `{distance}` plus
  `elsewhere` (Parkfield, 35.899722, -120.432778, Wikipedia).

## Conflicts and weak spots
- **Number of Parkfield earthquakes.** Caltrans says six on the Cholame/Parkfield segment "with the
  most recent earthquake in 2004"; USGS lists six from 1857 to 1966, then 2004 makes seven. The
  card lists the USGS years and does not give a count.
- **Where 1857 began.** Caltrans: epicentre "approximately near the Cholame area"; USGS: Sieh
  believed it "began near Parkfield". The card says "started near Cholame" with both cited; a
  checker might prefer "near Parkfield and Cholame".
- **Rupture length.** Caltrans: 20 miles northwest and 160 southeast. Wikipedia (Cholame): about
  300 km. The card uses Caltrans's 160 miles southeast.
- **Slip.** Cholame section 3 to 7 metres (USGS); maximum about 9 metres on the Carrizo Plain
  (USGS), "up to 30 feet" (Caltrans). The card says "in places ... about 30 feet".
- "Perhaps the largest historical quake in California" is Caltrans's hedge; the card keeps "perhaps".
- "Most closely observed earthquake zone in the world" (Wikipedia, Parkfield) is a single-source
  superlative; not used.
- The 4-inch 1966 pavement offset and the 11-foot potential offset are Caltrans prose measurements,
  not table figures; the 4 inches is used, the 11 feet is not.
